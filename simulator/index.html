🔐 Forward-Secure Signatures Simulator

An interactive visual guide to how the MMM scheme builds forward secrecy using ML-DSA

🏦 Meet SecureBank

SecureBank digitally signs every transaction approval. Customers, auditors, and regulators all rely on these signatures to trust that transactions are genuine.

The bank uses two keys:

  • Verification certificate (public key) — published openly so anyone can check whether a transaction approval is authentic
  • Signing seal (secret key) — locked in a secure vault, used by a bank officer to stamp and approve transactions

💡 Think of it like a wax seal on a royal decree. The seal itself is kept in a vault (secret key), but everyone knows what the seal looks like (public key) so they can verify the decree is real.

😱 The Nightmare Scenario

Imagine a hacker breaches SecureBank's vault and steals the signing seal.

With a normal signature scheme, the damage is catastrophic. The hacker can forge transaction approvals for any time — past, present, and future. Every transaction the bank has ever signed becomes untrustworthy. Auditors can no longer tell which approvals were genuine and which were forged.

💡 It's as if someone stole the only stamp the bank has ever used. They could fake approvals dated years ago, and no one could tell the difference.

🔑 The Solution: Forward Secrecy

SecureBank adopts a new policy: rotate the signing seal every week and permanently shred the old one.

Each week, a new officer takes over with a fresh seal. When their shift ends, the seal is destroyed — no one, not even the bank, can recreate it.

Now if a hacker steals this week's seal, they can only forge approvals for this week. All previous weeks are safe — those seals no longer exist. This is forward secrecy.

💡 It's like changing the lock on your vault every week and melting down the old key. A thief who grabs today's key can't open yesterday's lock — it's already gone.

🖥️ How This Simulator Works

SecureBank's digital seal is powered by ML-DSA — a post-quantum signing algorithm, meaning it's secure even against future quantum computers. But ML-DSA alone has no forward secrecy — one key, forever.

This simulator shows how the MMM scheme adds forward secrecy to ML-DSA using two building blocks:

  • Sum Composition — a relay of weekly signing officers who take turns and destroy their seals after each shift
  • Product Composition — organizing officers into quarters managed by a Branch Manager, adding two layers of protection

These combine into the full MMM scheme, where SecureBank can operate indefinitely — each quarter grows larger than the last. Click the tabs above to see each one animated step by step.
